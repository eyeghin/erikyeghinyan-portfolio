# Transaction-Monitoring Case Study

> Fictional scenario created for portfolio use. It contains no real customer or employer data.

## Alert

**Scenario:** Rapid movement of incoming ACH credits to multiple P2P recipients  
**Customer:** U.S. consumer checking account  
**Review period:** 30 days  
**Alert trigger:** A sharp increase in incoming ACH volume followed by same-day outbound P2P transfers

## Customer Profile

The account was opened eight months earlier. The customer stated that the account would be used for payroll, household purchases, and routine person-to-person transfers. Historical activity consisted of one biweekly payroll deposit, debit-card purchases, and occasional transfers to two recurring recipients.

## Activity Reviewed

During the review period, the account received eight ACH credits totaling $18,740 from five unrelated business originators. Approximately 91% of the funds were transferred within 24 hours to eleven P2P recipients. Several transfers used repeated round-dollar amounts, and six recipients had not appeared in the customer's prior activity. The account retained a low ending balance.

## Investigative Steps

1. Confirmed the alert logic and reviewed the complete transaction history.
2. Compared current activity with the customer's stated purpose and historical baseline.
3. Analyzed timing, velocity, values, channels, counterparties, and flow of funds.
4. Reviewed KYC information and available account-opening records.
5. Researched ACH originators and relevant counterparties using approved open sources.
6. Checked for prior alerts, customer contact, returned payments, disputes, and account-access anomalies.
7. Documented both risk indicators and potentially reasonable explanations.

## Key Risk Indicators

- Material deviation from the established account pattern
- Multiple unrelated ACH originators
- Rapid dissipation of incoming funds
- Newly introduced P2P counterparties
- Repeated round-dollar transfers
- Limited economic purpose visible from account activity
- Low balance retained after transfers

## Potential Mitigating Information

- No confirmed sanctions match
- No previously documented suspicious activity
- No conclusive public-source evidence connecting the customer to criminal activity
- Account takeover could not be concluded solely from the transactional pattern

## Disposition

**Escalate for enhanced review.**

The activity was inconsistent with the customer's expected use and showed rapid pass-through behavior involving multiple unrelated parties. The evidence did not establish an underlying offense, but the combination of velocity, counterparties, and lack of apparent purpose warranted additional review and possible customer outreach under applicable procedures.

## Documentation Principle

The case conclusion should describe what occurred, why it differed from expectations, what research was completed, and why escalation was reasonable—without overstating facts or alleging criminal conduct.
