# Payments & Fraud Typologies

## Why the Operating Model Matters

A traditional bank and a digital fintech may face many of the same financial-crime risks, but the signals can look different because customer acquisition, payment speed, product design, and intervention points differ.

| Dimension | Traditional bank | Digital fintech |
|---|---|---|
| Customer interaction | Branch and digital context | Primarily remote onboarding |
| Payment velocity | Often mixed across channels | Frequently real-time or near-real-time |
| Identity signals | Documents plus possible in-person contact | Device, behavioral, document, and digital identity data |
| Intervention | Staff may question activity before completion | Controls often depend on automated detection |
| Scaling challenge | Consistency across branches and systems | High alert volume and rapid customer growth |

## 1. Account Takeover

**Potential indicators**

- New device or location followed by payment activity
- Password, email, phone, or authentication changes
- New payees and rapid transfer velocity
- Uncharacteristic balance depletion
- Customer reports of lost access

**Analytical question:** Does the activity reflect financial-crime risk, unauthorized access, or legitimate customer behavior? The case may require coordination between fraud and AML teams.

## 2. Mule or Pass-Through Activity

**Potential indicators**

- Incoming funds from unrelated parties
- Rapid onward transfers
- Minimal balance retention
- Multiple payment rails
- Activity inconsistent with occupation or account purpose

A single fast transfer is not determinative. The pattern, counterparties, customer profile, and economic purpose matter.

## 3. ACH Fraud and Returns

**Potential indicators**

- Sudden increase in ACH credits or debits
- Unrelated originators
- Repeated returns or unauthorized claims
- Funds removed before a return is received
- New accounts with immediate high-value activity

## 4. P2P Scams

P2P activity can involve account takeover, impersonation, romance scams, purchase scams, or authorized push-payment fraud. Review recipient novelty, memo text, contact history, velocity, customer age or vulnerability indicators when permitted, and whether funds continue to other accounts.

## 5. Structuring

Repeated cash transactions below a reporting threshold can be relevant, but value alone is not enough. Consider timing, branches, individuals involved, stated purpose, historical behavior, and whether the transactions appear designed to avoid reporting or recordkeeping.

## 6. Synthetic Identity

**Potential indicators**

- Thin or inconsistent identity footprint
- Reused addresses, devices, or contact information
- Identity elements linked to multiple profiles
- Gradual credit-building followed by rapid exposure
- Document and database inconsistencies

## Operating Principle

Strong monitoring combines rules, models, contextual data, and human judgment. Automation helps prioritize risk; investigators determine whether the evidence supports closure, additional information, or escalation.
