ILLUSTRATIVE CASE NOTE01 / 03

Specific enough to act on.

01Scope

This fictional review covers two business days for a personal account whose stated expected funding is salaried employment.

02Activity

The account received three ACH credits of $6,200, $7,400, and $5,000 from separate businesses, totaling $18,600. Subsequent P2P payments of $11,300 and a transfer of $6,500 totaled $17,800, or 95.7% of the incoming value.

03Analysis

The available customer profile does not explain the relationships to the funding businesses or payment recipients. Rapid turnover and unexplained third-party funding warrant further review. The pattern alone does not establish money laundering.

04Disposition

Refer for supervisory BSA/AML review to establish economic purpose and counterparty relationships. Include the activity chronology, relevant customer records, and outstanding evidence requests for further assessment and SAR consideration, as appropriate.

CASE QUEUE04
FR-001 / Fraud / ATOSIMULATED CASE

New device. New payee. Rapid exit.

A customer with a stable payroll-and-bills profile signs in from a new device. Contact information changes, a payee is added, and two transfers follow within minutes.

REVIEW VALUE$4,850
TIMEFRAME18 minutes
PRIMARY SIGNALDevice + payment velocity

Activity timeline

TimeEventDetailValueReview
09:02AccessNew device sign-in—Review
09:06ProfilePhone number changed—Review
09:12P2PNew recipient transfer$2,400Flagged
09:20P2PSame recipient transfer$2,450Flagged

What is established

  • Two outbound transfers total $4,850 within eight minutes.
  • Device, contact, and beneficiary changes occur in the same session.
  • The previous 60 days show no payments to this recipient.

What I still need to know

  • Can the customer be verified through an established, trusted channel?
  • Is the device change explained by a legitimate replacement?
  • Are pending transfers or related accounts exposed?
