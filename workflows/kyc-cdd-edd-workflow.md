# Risk-Based KYC, CDD & EDD Workflow

This workflow illustrates my understanding of customer due diligence. Exact requirements depend on an institution's policies, products, jurisdiction, and regulatory obligations.

## 1. Customer Identification

- Collect required identifying information.
- Validate documents and data using approved sources.
- Resolve material discrepancies before proceeding.
- Record the evidence used and the result.

## 2. Customer Profile

Understand:

- Occupation or business activity
- Purpose of the account
- Expected products and transaction types
- Anticipated volume, value, counterparties, and geography
- Source of funds or wealth when relevant
- Ownership and control structure for legal entities

## 3. Screening

Complete applicable:

- Sanctions and watchlist screening
- PEP identification
- Adverse-media research
- Internal negative-list or prior-relationship checks
- Beneficial-owner screening

A potential name match is not automatically a true match. Compare identifiers such as full name, date of birth, location, nationality, address, and other available data.

## 4. Risk Assessment

Evaluate risk across:

| Factor | Examples |
|---|---|
| Customer | Occupation, entity type, ownership complexity |
| Product | Cash access, wires, P2P, international capability |
| Geography | Customer location, counterparties, sanctioned or higher-risk jurisdictions |
| Channel | Branch, online-only, intermediated, non-face-to-face |
| Expected activity | Volume, value, purpose, counterparties |
| Screening | PEP, adverse media, sanctions-related indicators |

Document the rationale, not only the final rating.

## 5. Enhanced Due Diligence

EDD may be appropriate when the initial review identifies elevated or unexplained risk. Depending on policy, this can include:

- Additional identity or ownership evidence
- Source-of-funds or source-of-wealth information
- Explanation and support for expected activity
- Deeper adverse-media and counterparty research
- Senior or specialist approval
- More frequent review or targeted monitoring

## 6. Decision

Possible outcomes include:

- Approve under standard monitoring
- Approve with conditions or enhanced monitoring
- Request additional information
- Escalate to AML, sanctions, fraud, or legal specialists
- Decline or exit according to approved policy

## 7. Ongoing Due Diligence

Customer risk changes. Review trigger events such as:

- Significant changes in transaction behavior
- New products, geographies, or counterparties
- Ownership or occupation changes
- New adverse media or screening results
- Repeated fraud claims, returns, or unexplained activity
- Periodic-review deadlines

## First and Second Lines

Frontline bankers help execute customer-identification controls, understand transaction context, recognize red flags, and escalate concerns. Specialized compliance functions provide independent oversight, risk frameworks, and challenge. Clear handoffs between these responsibilities improve both customer service and risk management.
